# Revenue: hybrid, phased, with cash always free

ReMatch earns money in phases (model D in `docs/research/monetization.md`, compared in the throwaway prototype kept on branch `prototype/revenue-models`):

1. **Phase 1:** a Platform fee on online Charges of the Provider's cost plus 1%, with a minimum of Rp4.000 or 4% of the Charge, whichever is lower. The Host or Organizer chooses whether the payer or the Payee absorbs it. Organizer/Club Pro (about Rp49k–99k/month) and Season sponsorship.
2. **Phase 2:** player Premium (about Rp29k–49k/month).
3. **Phase 3:** Venue software (about Rp0.5–1.5 jt/month) with no commission on the Venue's own Customers.

Cash marks never carry a fee, so ReMatch competes with WhatsApp and bank transfer instead of fighting them. The minimum scales down for small Charges because a badminton mabar slot is about Rp28.000, where a flat Rp4.000–5.500 would take 14–20%.

## Considered Options

- **Venue software first:** steady, but needs venue sales city by city, earns nothing at public courts, and AYO Indonesia already sells it with no commission.
- **Marketplace fees (Playtomic-style 4%, minimum Rp5.500):** earns most per padel game, but is heavy on badminton and loses everything that moves to cash.
- **Subscriptions only:** easiest start, but low revenue per user in Indonesia, and users react badly to surprise auto-renewals.

## Consequences

- Fees must be published up front and existing users grandfathered when they change. Meetup, Strava and AYO all drew backlash for adding or raising fees later.
- Premium renewals need loud reminders and one-tap cancel.
